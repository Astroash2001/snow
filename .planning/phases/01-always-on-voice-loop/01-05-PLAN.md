1. `scripts/make_audio_fixtures.py` synthesizes speech with kokoro-onnx 0.6.1 (pinned in plan 01-01; model files from the kokoro-onnx GitHub release `model-files-v1.0` downloaded once into the gitignored `models/kokoro/`), resamples to 16 kHz mono int16 (`soxr` or numpy, whichever the lock already provides), assembles silence gaps with numpy and writes the committed WAVs with `soundfile`. Fixed seeds and fixed text so reruns are byte-stable where the engine allows.

2. Fixture set (the wake phrase is the hey_jarvis stand-in until plan 01-30 trains "hey snow", D-41): `wake_only` = "Hey Jarvis" then 3 s silence (D-09 wake-alone); `wake_cmd_gap0`, `wake_cmd_gap150`, `wake_cmd_gap600` = "Hey Jarvis" + 0, 150 or 600 ms gap + "what is the capital of France?" (D-09 one-breath command, pre-roll); `mid_thought_pause` = "Hey Jarvis, I was thinking that we could" + 1.2 s pause + "maybe check the weather tomorrow." (VOIC-05); `tv_negative` = 30 s of varied sentences without the wake phrase plus low noise; `phrase_thats_all` = "Hey Jarvis, that's all" (D-08); `phrase_mute` = "Hey Jarvis, mute" and `phrase_go_to_sleep` = "Hey Jarvis, go to sleep" (D-10); `phrase_mute_substring` = "Hey Jarvis, mute the speaker in the call"; `conversation_two_turns` = "Hey Jarvis, what time is it?" + 2 s silence + "and what day is it?"; `tone_calm` (kokoro speed 0.85, gain -10 dB) and `tone_stressed` (speed 1.35, gain +6 dB) with the same sentence and no wake phrase (D-35). Pick a kokoro voice whose synthetic "Hey Jarvis" scores at least 0.7 on hey_jarvis and record the voice name in the script. Fixtures are synthetic, never the user's voice.

3. `--check` mode (no rewriting): every listed file exists, is 16 kHz, mono, int16, and its duration falls within the expected range per fixture; running openWakeWord `hey_jarvis` (ONNX inference, model downloaded once by `openwakeword.utils.download_models`) over each `wake_*`, `phrase_*`, `mid_thought_pause` and `conversation_two_turns` file gives a maximum score of at least 0.7, and over `tv_negative` a maximum below 0.5. It prints one line per file and exits 1 on any failure.
